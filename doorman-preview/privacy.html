Privacy policy

Last updated 23 November 2026. Doorman is made by Survive Labs.

The short version

Doorman runs on your own device. We don't collect, store, sell or send anything you check with it. There's no account and no tracking, and Doorman never shows adverts.

What happens when you use it

  • Messages and links you check are checked by code running in your browser. They're never sent to us or anyone else, and Doorman doesn't open the links.
  • Photos are read in your browser to find hidden details such as location. With Pro, a clean copy is redrawn in your browser. Your photo never leaves your device.
  • Screenshots (Pro) are read by text-recognition software that's built into the extension and runs on your device.
  • QR codes and attachments are read on your device. Attachments are never opened or run.
  • Scan this page: only when you click it, Doorman reads that page's address, text and links, and whether it has password or card boxes, to look for warning signs. Nothing is kept afterwards.
  • Always-on and Marketplace Guard (Pro): when you switch them on, Doorman checks each page you open in the same way, on your device. It notices when you click into a password, card or bank box on a risky page so it can warn you, but it never reads, stores or sends what you type.

The known scam websites list (Pro)

Once a day, Doorman Pro downloads a list of reported phishing websites. It's a download only: your browser asks for the file and nothing about you, your browsing or the sites you visit is sent. The list is kept on your device and every lookup happens there. The file is built from public lists, including Phishing.Database (MIT licence), and is hosted on GitHub, who will see the request for the file as they would for any download.

Alerts (Pro, optional)

If you switch on alerts, Doorman can show a notification on your computer when a page in another tab shows strong signs of a scam. The notification is created on your device.

What's stored on your device

  • Your settings: which Pro features are on, and the sites you've told Doorman to trust.
  • The known scam websites list (Pro) and the date it was downloaded.
  • A list of well-known websites, built into the extension, so popular sites don't cause false alarms.

Removing the extension deletes all of these.

Permissions the Chrome extension uses

  • activeTab and scripting: to check the page you're on when you click the button.
  • contextMenus: to add "Ask the Doorman" to the right-click menu.
  • storage: to keep your settings and lists on your device.
  • alarms: to fetch the known scam websites list once a day (Pro).
  • Access to websites (optional): only asked for when you switch on Always-on or Marketplace Guard, so Doorman can check pages as you open them.
  • Notifications (optional): only asked for if you switch on alerts.

This website

This site doesn't use cookies or tracking. Its fonts are hosted here rather than fetched from another company. The checker on this site runs in your browser tab, and nothing you paste is sent anywhere.

Payments

When Pro launches, payments will be handled by Stripe. We'll receive your email address and payment status so we can give you your Pro licence. We never see your card details.

Its limits

Doorman looks for common warning signs. It can't prove something is safe, and it can miss scams. If you're unsure, contact the company using a number or website you already know.
