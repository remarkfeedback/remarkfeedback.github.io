How to make a comment bank in Google Docs

A comment bank is the feedback you give most often, written once and reused. Here's how to build a good one, three free ways to use it in Google Docs, and a starter bank you can copy today.

About a 6-minute read · For teachers marking in Google Docs or Google Classroom

1

Write comments worth reusing

Mark five pieces of work as normal, then copy every comment you wrote into one list. Delete duplicates and you'll usually have 15 to 25 comments that cover most of what you say.

Then improve each one:

  • Say what to do next. "Explain how this quotation proves your point" beats "needs more analysis".
  • Keep it to one idea. Two short comments are easier to reuse than one long one.
  • Make praise specific. "Your topic sentence makes your argument clear" tells the student what to keep doing; "good" doesn't.
  • Group them under headings such as Praise, Next steps, Literacy and your subject skills, so you can find them fast.

Three free ways to use it in Google Docs

A. A second tab. Keep your bank in its own Google Doc, open in another tab or window. Copy a comment, select the student's words, press Ctrl+Alt+M (Cmd+Option+M on a Mac) and paste. Simple, and it works everywhere.

B. Google Classroom's comment bank. If you grade in Classroom, open a student's work, click Comment bank on the right and paste your list in, one comment per line. When you type a comment, matching ones from your bank pop up.

C. Shortcuts with Substitutions. In Google Docs, go to Tools → Preferences → Substitutions. Add a short code such as ev1 and set it to replace with "Explain how your evidence proves your point." Type ev1 and a space in the document and the full comment appears. It works when typing in the document itself, so it suits feedback written into the work or a summary at the end.

Want your bank in a sidebar instead?

Remark is a Google Docs add-on that keeps your comment bank right next to the essay. Select some words, click a comment, and it's added as a highlighted comment addressed to the student by name. It also has marking codes with an automatic key, and a WWW, EBI and DIRT summary box.

Questions teachers ask

How many comments should a comment bank have?

Start with 15 to 25. A bank of 200 takes longer to search than writing the comment. Add new ones only when you've typed the same thing three times.

Can I share a comment bank with my department?

Yes. Keep it in a shared Google Doc so everyone can copy from it and suggest additions. Shared wording also helps students see consistent feedback across classes.

Is reusing comments lazy?

No. It frees up time for the one or two comments on each piece that need writing from scratch. Adding the student's name keeps it personal.
