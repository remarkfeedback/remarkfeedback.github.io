How to mark essays faster in Google Docs

Thirty essays, the same five mistakes, and you're writing "explain how your evidence proves your point" for the twentieth time. Here are practical ways to give students good written feedback in Google Docs, in a lot less time.

About a 7-minute read · For teachers marking work in Google Docs or Google Classroom

On this page
  1. Build a comment bank
  2. Use marking codes
  3. End with WWW, EBI and a DIRT task
  4. Speed up the clicking
  5. Questions teachers ask

None of these ideas are about marking less carefully. They're about not rewriting the same sentence thirty times, so your time goes into the comments that need real thought.

1

Build a comment bank

A comment bank is a list of the feedback you give most often, written once and reused. Start by marking five essays as normal, then copy every comment you wrote into one document. Most teachers find the same 15 to 25 comments cover the bulk of what they say.

Group them so they're quick to find:

  • Literacy and SPaG: spelling, punctuation, paragraphing, sentence structure.
  • Skills for your subject: using evidence, explaining analysis, linking to the question.
  • Praise: specific, so it tells the student what to keep doing.

Write each comment so it tells the student what to do next, such as "Explain how your quotation proves your point". "Needs more analysis" says what's missing but not how to fix it.

If you use Google Classroom, its grading tool has a built-in comment bank. Open a student's work, click Comment bank on the right, and paste in your list with one comment per line. Classroom keeps it with your Google account, so it's there for every class.

2

Use marking codes

For small, repeated errors, a short code in the margin is faster than a full comment and teaches students to spot their own mistakes. Common codes in UK schools include:

CodeMeaning
SpSpelling mistake: check and correct this word
PPunctuation error
CCapital letter needed (or used wrongly)
//Start a new paragraph here
EvAdd evidence or an example here
ExExplain this point further

Codes only work if students know what they mean, so put a key at the end of each piece of work or on the classroom wall. We've made a free printable marking codes poster and comment bank (PDF) you're welcome to use in your school.

3

End with WWW, EBI and a DIRT task

Finish each piece with a short summary rather than a long paragraph:

  • WWW (What Went Well): one specific strength.
  • EBI (Even Better If): the single most important next step.
  • DIRT (Directed Improvement and Reflection Time): one small task the student completes in the next lesson, such as "Rewrite paragraph 2, explaining one quotation in detail."

The DIRT task is what turns feedback into progress. A student who rewrites one paragraph using your EBI learns more than one who reads three paragraphs of comments.

4

Speed up the clicking

  • Keyboard shortcut for comments: select the words and press Ctrl+Alt+M (Cmd+Option+M on a Mac).
  • Mark one question at a time across the whole class rather than one whole essay at a time. You hold the mark scheme in your head and reuse the same comments in a row.
  • Keep the comment bank open in a second tab or window so copying a comment is two clicks.
  • Use the student's name. "Jordan, explain how this quotation proves your point" feels personal, even when the rest of the comment is from your bank.

Questions teachers ask

Isn't reusing comments less personal?

Not if the comment is the right one for that student. The time you save on repeated comments is time you can spend on the one or two that need writing from scratch. Adding the student's name helps too.

How many comments should I leave on an essay?

Fewer than you think. Two or three focused comments and one clear next step usually do more than marking every error. Marking codes handle the small stuff without cluttering the page.

Do students actually read feedback in Google Docs?

More often when they have to act on it. Giving time in the next lesson to respond to a DIRT task is the most reliable way to make sure feedback gets used.
