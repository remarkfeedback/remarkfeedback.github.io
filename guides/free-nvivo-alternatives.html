Free and cheap NVivo alternatives for students

NVivo is the name everyone mentions, but you don't have to buy it to code your interviews. Here are the free and low-cost options, what each is good for, and how to choose one for your dissertation.

Do you actually need NVivo?

Probably not. NVivo, ATLAS.ti and MAXQDA are powerful, but your examiner marks the quality of your analysis, not the software you used. Thematic analysis is a method, and plenty of good dissertations are coded with highlighters, Word or Google Docs.

Specialist software earns its price when you have a lot of data (say 30 or more long interviews), audio or video to code directly, or a team coding together. For a typical undergraduate or master's dissertation with 6 to 15 interviews, a simpler tool is usually enough, and it's one less thing to learn in a busy term.

Check what your university already gives you

Before you pay for anything, look on your university's IT or software pages. Many UK universities have a site licence for NVivo that students can install on their own laptops for free while they're enrolled. Search your IT pages for "NVivo" or "qualitative software".

Two things to watch for: some site licences only work while you're in the UK or Ireland, and your access usually ends when you leave. If you'll finish your write-up after your course ends, make sure you can export your work.

Free alternatives

1

Taguette

Free and open source. You upload documents (Word, PDF, text and more), highlight passages and tag them with codes, then export your highlights and codebook. It runs in your browser: install it on your computer, or register for the free online version at app.taguette.org, which also lets you work with others.

Good for: simple highlight-and-tag coding. Less good for: complex queries or audio and video.

2

QualCoder

Free, open-source desktop software for Windows, Mac and Linux. It's closer to the paid packages: you can code text, images, audio and video, write memos, organise codes into categories and run reports such as code frequencies.

Good for: students who want NVivo-style features for free. Less good for: anyone who wants something they can learn in an afternoon.

3

Google Docs or Word, by hand

Use highlight colours or comments for codes, and keep a codebook table in a separate document. Then copy quotes into a table grouped by theme. It costs nothing and works anywhere, but copying quotes around gets slow once you have more than a handful of interviews. Our step-by-step guide to thematic analysis in Google Docs shows how.

4

Themely (our add-on for Google Docs)

We make Themely, so take this with a pinch of salt. It adds coding to Google Docs: highlight a quote, click a code, and Themely collects every quote under its theme across your interviews, then builds a findings report and quote table. Your transcripts stay in your own Google account. It's free for 3 interviews, with a £9 Dissertation Pass for six months. See how Themely works.

Good for: dissertations in Google Docs. Less good for: audio and video, or very large projects. It's free on the Google Workspace Marketplace for up to 3 interviews.

How to choose

  • Your university has NVivo and your supervisor uses it: use NVivo. Help is easier to find.
  • Text interviews, under about 20, and you want to start today: a lightweight tool such as Taguette, Themely, or Google Docs by hand.
  • Audio or video you want to code without transcribing, for free: QualCoder.
  • Coding with other people: something online that you can share, such as Taguette's online version or Google Docs.

Whatever you pick: say in your methods section which software you used and how. Examiners care that your coding was systematic and transparent, not which brand did it.

Questions people ask

Is there a free version of NVivo?

No. NVivo has a free trial, and some universities give students free access through a site licence, but there's no permanently free version. Free alternatives include Taguette and QualCoder.

Will my examiner mind if I don't use NVivo?

Very unlikely. Examiners mark how well you analysed your data and how clearly you explain your method. Many strong dissertations are coded by hand or in a word processor. If in doubt, ask your supervisor.

Can I do thematic analysis in Google Docs?

Yes. You can code with highlights or comments, keep a codebook table, and group quotes by theme. Our guide shows each step, and Themely can speed up the coding and quote-collecting parts.

What's the easiest free qualitative analysis software?

For simple highlight-and-tag coding of text, Taguette is one of the quickest to learn because it works like highlighting on paper. QualCoder has more features but takes longer to learn.
