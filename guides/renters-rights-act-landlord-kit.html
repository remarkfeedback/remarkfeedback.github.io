What changed on 1 May 2026

The Renters' Rights Act 2025 ended assured shorthold tenancies and fixed terms for new private lettings in England. New tenancies are now open-ended assured periodic tenancies.

Before you agree a new tenancy, you must give each tenant a written statement of terms: the key facts about the tenancy and their rights. GOV.UK says you can be fined up to £7,000 if you don't. You can put this information in your tenancy agreement or give it separately, and you don't have to use the exact legal wording.

In Wales or Scotland? This kit isn't for you. Wales uses occupation contracts under the Renting Homes (Wales) Act, and Scotland has its own rules.

Your checklist

  • New tenancy since 1 May 2026Give every tenant the written statement before you sign or agree the tenancy. Use the builder below or your own agreement, as long as it covers everything required.
  • Tenancy started before 1 May 2026, with a written agreementYou needed to give each tenant the government's official Information Sheet by 31 May 2026. It must be the exact PDF from GOV.UK, not a link or your own summary. If you missed it, do it now and consider taking advice.
  • Tenancy started before 1 May 2026, with only a spoken agreementThose tenants needed a full written statement by 31 May 2026. If you haven't given one, the builder below covers the same information.
  • Putting the rent upRent increases now go through a formal notice under section 13 of the Housing Act 1988. Your statement has to tell tenants this.
  • PetsTenants can ask to keep a pet, and you can't unreasonably refuse. This is also part of the statement.
  • The landlord databaseRegistration opens region by region from 15 December 2026, and every landlord in England must be registered by 14 November 2027.

Questions

Is this a full tenancy agreement?

No. It builds the written statement of terms, which is the information the law says a tenant must get before an assured periodic tenancy is agreed. You can give it on its own or attach it to your tenancy agreement. Any other terms you add must comply with the law.

Do I have to use this wording?

No. GOV.UK says you don't need to use the exact wording in the legislation, as long as you give all the required information. This builder follows the list in the official regulations, written in plain English.

Does the address for notices have to be my home?

The rules ask for an address in England and Wales where your tenant can serve notices on you, including court papers. They don't say it must be your home, but it must be somewhere notices will reliably reach you. If you're unsure, take advice before you use a different address.

I let through an agent. Do I need this?

Your agent will usually prepare it, but you're still responsible for it being right. It's worth checking their version against this list.

Is this legal advice?

No. We've built it carefully from the official sources below, but every tenancy is different. If your situation is unusual, such as a lodger, a company let or a property in Wales, get advice.
