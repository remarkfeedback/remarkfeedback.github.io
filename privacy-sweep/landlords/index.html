What's happening, and when

The Renters' Rights Act brings in a national database of private landlords in England. Here are the dates that matter.

  1. 15 Dec 2026
    Registration opens

    It rolls out one region a month, so your turn could come soon after.

  2. Winter 2027
    Earliest public access

    The government says nothing will be publicly visible before winter 2027. Later rules will decide what the public can see.

  3. 14 Nov 2027
    Final deadline

    Every landlord in England must be registered. Councils can fine those who aren't.

Questions landlords ask

Will my home address be public on the landlord database?

Not for now. GOV.UK says landlord and property information will not currently be shared publicly, and that some information may be viewable to the public in future, with a privacy notice before any change. The register is separate from what's already online: your name is on every tenancy agreement, and for many landlords that's enough to find a home address. That's the part we check.

Do I have to register?

If you let a home privately in England, yes. Registration opens on 15 December 2026, one region a month, and every landlord has to be registered by 14 November 2027. Councils can fine landlords who don't register. Wales and Scotland have their own landlord registration schemes.

Can I register with an address that isn't my home?

You register directly with GOV.UK and give the details it asks for, including a correspondence address in England or Wales. Giving a correspondence address doesn't mean your home address is kept out of the government's records, and we don't change what you give the government. Check the GOV.UK guidance when your region opens. We don't give legal advice.

Where else is my address likely to be?

Often in more places than you'd think: paperwork you've given tenants, certificates, property and company records, people-search websites and old letting adverts. Your report lists the places we find it, ranked by risk.

Is this legal?

Yes. UK data protection law gives you the right to ask companies to delete your personal data, and lets someone act for you with your written permission. Some records are public by law, and we'll tell you which ones before you pay for removals.

I let through an agent. Do I still need this?

Your agent may handle tenants, but your own details can still be on public records and websites, and every landlord still has to register. The sweep looks at you, not the agent.

How long does it take?

Your report arrives within 5 working days of your form and permission letter coming back. With the £69 service, companies have up to a month to act on a removal request, which they can extend in complicated cases. We chase any that don't reply for up to 3 months after we send the requests, then send you a final report.

What if you find nothing to remove?

Then you get your money back. If your report finds nothing of yours that can be removed, we refund your £19 in full. If you booked the £69 service and there turns out to be nothing to remove, we refund that in full too.

What if you find very little?

Your report says so, and shows what we checked. That's still worth knowing before you register. We won't suggest removals you don't need.

What happens if a site refuses to remove something?

We tell you why, whether the law requires them to act, and what else you can do. Some records, such as property ownership and sales, are public by law and can't be removed.

How long do you keep my details?

Our working notes and screenshots are deleted 30 days after your final report. Your form, permission letter, report and request records are generally kept for 12 months after your service ends. Read the full privacy policy.

Can I cancel?

Yes. Cancel within 14 days of booking and we'll refund you in full if we haven't started your search. If we have started at your request, we'll refund the part we haven't done yet. Email hello@survivelabs.co.uk.
